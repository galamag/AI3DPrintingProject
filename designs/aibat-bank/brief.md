Status: approved by the user on 2026-11-13; outline per side chosen by the user from the renders of the symmetric version.

**Revisions of 2026-11-13.** The user changed the first draft four times on the same day. This brief includes all four.

1. **Name on both sides.** The user said that the inscription must be on two sides, that this is why the box is printed as two separate parts, and that on the other side the inscription is placed as a mirror image so that whoever looks at the box, from either side, reads АЙБАТ left to right. There is no plain back wall. The back half's outside is the front half's outside turned half a turn (180 degrees) round the vertical centre line. The 80 mm depth is measured from letter faces to letter faces, and the split is in the middle (Y 0), so the halves are 40 + 40 mm.
2. **Straight letters, printed with supports.** No 45 degree bevel. Each side is a flat black face, and the green letters stand straight out of it by 4.0 mm, with upright walls. Each half prints letters down, so the flat black face is printed over supports. It will not have the plate texture and will show support marks; the user accepted this on 2026-11-13 in exchange for straight letters. The user's own point: the supports are printed during the green layers, so they come out green and cost no extra filament change or purge.
3. **Colour change two layers early.** The filament changes to black two layers before the flat black face starts, so the black face is not smeared with green from the supports. With a 4.0 mm relief and 0.2 mm layers the change is at 3.6 mm.
4. **The outline follows the letters (fourth change).** The user saw renders of the version with one shared, left-right symmetric outline and a flat plateau on top, and chose instead that each side's outline follows its own letters, as in the reference photos. The user was told beforehand that this leaves steps along the seam where the two outlines do not match. What changed:
   - Each half's outside follows its own word, grown by 8 mm: the hump over Й, the leaning А end, the step-in under Т's arm, and the dips between letters. There is no plateau, no filled end wedge and no mirrored union.
   - The inside and the joint follow the **common shape**, the part the two outlines share. It is symmetric left to right, so the hollow, lip, rebate, click ridges and grooves are exactly as designed, and the test piece is unchanged.
   - Where one half's outline reaches beyond the common shape, that half is solid there and ends at the seam in a flat ledge (a visible flat step in the assembled box).
   - The coin slot sits on the short flat top over Б that both halves share: 32 mm long instead of 38 mm.
   - Two of the four click bumps moved from the top wall to the end walls, because the common top has no clear flat stretch for them.
   - Real measurements from the built model replace the estimates for the letters (section 2).

Unchanged by the fourth change: the size 220 x 100 x 80 mm, the split at Y 0, the 4.0 mm straight relief, the colour change at the relief height minus two layers, the supports under the black face, the 3.2 mm walls, the lip on the front half, the colours and the test piece.

The user fixed these points: the name «АЙБАТ» in bold Cyrillic capitals like the THOMAS reference, on both sides of the box, readable from each side, with the box's outline following the letters; an overall size of 220 x 100 x 80 mm; green letters on a black body; a box for coins up to about 26 mm and folded banknotes; renders before printing; and a real print. ## 1. What it is

A personalised money box in the style of the name banks in the references. It is a thick black body whose outline follows the name «АЙБАТ», with the name as raised green capitals on both the front and the back. Each side is a flat black face, and the five letters stand straight out of it by 4 mm, with upright walls and flat faces, like the THOMAS reference. Round the letters is an 8 mm black border that follows them: a hump over the short mark on Й, a leaning end beside the first А, a step in under the right arm of Т, and small dips in the top between letters. The style is clean and toy-like: flat colours, crisp edges. A coin slot sits on top. The box splits down the middle into a front half and a back half, which click together and can be pried apart with a coin to take the money out.

**Two outlines, one box.** Seen from behind, the name runs the other way round the box. The first А is at the end where Т is on the front, and the hump over Й is on the other side of the centre. Each half follows its own name, so the two halves differ in outline. Where they differ, one half stands out past the other at the seam and ends there in a flat step. From the front or the back, each side looks like the reference photos. From the top and the ends, the steps along the seam show (section 3.1).

**Surface finish, as accepted by the user.** Each half prints with its letter faces down on the textured plate. The green letter faces therefore get the fine plate texture. The flat black face is printed in mid-air, 4 mm above the plate, on supports that are snapped off afterwards. It comes out slightly rough and shows faint support marks, and it has no plate texture. The side walls and top show fine horizontal layer lines. The flat steps at the seam are the top of each half as printed, so they come out smooth.

Assumptions I made where the references and the user's answers say nothing:

- **Font: Impact**, which is installed on this PC (`C:\Windows\Fonts\impact.ttf`) and has Cyrillic letters. It is the closest installed match to the THOMAS letters: upright, heavy and narrow. Fitted to 204 mm wide it needs only a slight upward stretch (1.051 times, measured), so it keeps its natural look.
- **Relief height 4.0 mm.** That is 20 layers of 0.2 mm. On 69 mm tall capitals it reads clearly from the side and in the three-quarter view and casts a visible shadow. A higher relief would make the supports taller, use more filament and make them harder to pull out of the letter holes; a lower one would look flat.
- **The 100 mm height is the top of the hump over Й.** The rest of the top is at 85 mm (84.96, measured).
- **The 80 mm depth is measured from the front letter faces to the back letter faces**, so it includes both 4 mm reliefs.
- **The back name is the front name turned half a turn round the vertical centre line.** From behind, the first А is on the viewer's left, as it is from the front. The two sides therefore look identical.
- **The name is centred by its width.** On each side the green word, from the left foot of the first А (X −102.00) to the right tip of Т's top bar (X +102.00), is centred on the box.
- **The dips in the top between letters are kept.** Next to each А's flat top, the grown outline dips down in a V about 17 mm wide (estimate) and about 10–13 mm deep (estimate; the modeler measures). They are part of the outline following the letters.
- **The parts of a half that stand out past the common shape are solid**, with no hidden pockets (section 3.1 says why).
- **A small edge break** (a 0.6 mm 45 degree chamfer) runs round the outer edge of each flat black face and round the outer edge of each seam ledge.
- **The coin slot is 32 mm long** on the shared flat top over Б (section 3.2). A longer slot across the top is offered as an option.
- **Letters do not touch.** They have a 3 mm gap (measured), which shows as a thin black line of face.
- **The coin:** 200 tenge, about 26 mm across (the user's figure). I assume no coin is thicker than 2.5 mm.
- **The banknote** is folded lengthwise into thirds or quarters before it goes in, to a strip no wider than about 28 mm. This assumes a banknote about 70 mm tall, which is my estimate. A note folded only in half no longer fits the 32 mm slot.
- **The box is opened from below.** You turn it over, put a coin into a small notch at the bottom seam and twist.

## 3. Parts (largest to smallest)

The box is **2 printed parts**: the front half and the back half. A separate **test piece of the joint** has 4 small printed parts and is printed first. The scene therefore holds 8 objects in three groups. A "collection" is Blender's folder of objects. Supports are not modelled; the slicer adds them.

| Collection | Objects | What for |
|---|---|---|
| `AibatBank` | `BankFront`, `BankBack` | the assembled box, for the renders and checks |
| `AibatBank_Print` | `BankFront_Print`, `BankBack_Print` | the same two halves turned the way they lie on the print plate, for export and the print-orientation renders |
| `AibatBank_Coupon` | `CouponFront`, `CouponBack_015`, `CouponBack_020`, `CouponBack_025` | the joint test piece, already in print orientation |

`BankFront` and `BankBack` have their origins at the world origin (the centre of the bottom face) and no rotation, so they sit together without moving. The `_Print` objects are independent full copies, not linked copies. They are made only once the halves are final, and made again after any change. Each lies with its letter faces on Z = 0, at X +300 mm or beyond, so it never overlaps the assembled box.

### 3.1 The outlines: each half's own, and the common shape

**Each half's own outline.** This is the shape every slice of that half follows from its flat black face to the seam.

1. **Letters.** «АЙБАТ» in Impact capitals, spaced so every pair of neighbours is 3.0 mm apart at its closest point, the breve in its own position over Й. Fitted as on the built model: scaled evenly to 204 mm wide, stretched upwards 1.051 times to 84 mm from the baseline to the top of the breve, baseline at Z 8, centred on X 0 (X −102.00 to +102.00).
2. **Grow** the outline of all the letters, breve included, outwards by 8 mm, with round corners.
3. **Fill** every hole inside it: the insides of А and Б, closed gaps between letters (for example under Т's left arm), and the gap between the breve and Й.
4. **Cut** it flat at Z = 0, and fill any dip in the bottom edge (under the legs of each А) up to that flat line.
5. **Small fixes**, and nothing else:
   - the top of the hump is made one straight line at Z 100 between its two rounded shoulders, in case the breve's curved top leaves a shallow dip there. That keeps the hump's top and its seam ledge clean;
   - any notch in the outline that is narrower than 6 mm at its mouth is filled (none is expected);
   - sharp inside corners get a 2 mm round.
6. **Kept as they are:** the hump over Й, the lean of the А end, the step-in under Т's right arm, and the V-shaped dips in the top next to each А's flat top.

Growing an outline outwards makes no thin spikes or slivers, so there is nothing fragile to fill. Every outline edge is an upright wall when the half lies on the plate, so nothing in the outline is unprintable either.

The front half uses this outline as seen from the front. The back half uses the same outline as seen from behind, which in world coordinates is the front outline mirrored about X = 0. So the back half is still the front half turned half a turn round the vertical line X 0, Y 0.

**The front half's outline in plain words** (the back half's looks the same from behind):
- flat bottom from about X −102 to about X +90;
- the left end leans with the side of А, from X −110 just above the bottom to about X −96 at the top (estimate);
- top at Z 85 over А's flat top;
- a V dip;
- the hump over Й, 15 mm higher (Z 100), about 39 mm wide at its base and 23 mm across its flat top;
- top at Z 85 over Б;
- a V dip, Z 85 over the second А, another V dip;
- top at Z 85 along Т's top bar to the right end at X +110;
- the right end drops straight down past the bar and then steps in by about 11–12 mm under Т's arm, down to the bottom.

**The common shape** is the part both outlines share: the front outline and its mirror image, overlapped, keeping only where both are present. It is symmetric left to right. Its top is at Z 85 over Б (roughly X −17 to +17) and over the А flat tops near the ends, and it takes the lower of the two tops everywhere else, including every dip from both halves. Its ends are the inner of the two ends: about X ±98 up to about Z 55, then leaning out with the А side above that. Its bottom is flat from about X −90 to +90.

**What is built on the common shape** (both halves, so it matches across the seam):
- the hollow (the common shape shrunk by 3.2 mm);
- the front half's lip with its click ridges;
- the back half's rebate, skirt and grooves;
- the pry notch and the coin slot;
- the 0.6 mm seam bevel where the two halves are flush.

**Proud regions, and the rule for them.** Where a half's own outline reaches beyond the common shape, that half is **solid** out to its own outline, for the full 36 mm from its black face to the seam. There are no pockets.
- The biggest proud region is the hump: about 560 mm² in front view and 36 mm deep, about 20 cm³.
- The slicer already fills solid regions with light sparse infill, so a hidden pocket would save only a few grams.
- A pocket would also need either a ceiling just under the seam ledge (a bridge in mid-air when printed) or an open hole in the visible ledge.

**Seam ledges.** At the seam (Y 0), each proud region ends in a flat face square to the seam, facing the other half. It is part of the half's seam face, in the same plane. Its outer edge, where it meets the half's own side or top, gets the 0.6 mm 45 degree edge break. Its inner edge meets the other half's surface in a plain inside corner. In the assembled box these are visible flat steps.

### 3.2 `BankFront` (front half, with the front name and the lip)

**The front, from the letter faces back:**

- **Letter faces:** all five letters and the breve are flat, at Y −40, and parallel to the black face.
- **Letter walls:** each letter outline (and its holes) runs straight back, square to the face, for exactly 4.0 mm, to the flat black face at Y −36. No bevel, no slope, no rounding on the letters; the letter face edges are crisp.
- **Colour boundary:** there must be a real edge in the mesh at Y −36.4, 3.6 mm behind the letter faces, all round every letter wall and every hole wall. The letter faces and the walls in front of this edge are green; the last 0.4 mm of the walls behind it, and everything further back, is black. This is where the filament changes in the print (section 10).
- **Flat black face:** one flat surface at Y −36, filling the front half's own outline round the letters: the 8 mm border, the 3 mm gaps between letters, the insides of А and Б, and the gap between the breve and Й. It meets the letter walls in sharp inside corners.
- **Edge break:** round the outer edge of the flat black face, where it meets the side walls, a 0.6 mm 45 degree chamfer.
- **Sides:** the front half's own outline runs straight back from the black face to the seam at Y 0. On the common shape the wall is 3.2 mm thick; in the proud regions it is solid out to the outline (3.1).
- **Inside:**
  - **Shape:** the hollow is the common shape shrunk by 3.2 mm, with corners rounded to at least 2 mm. Any part of the hollow narrower than 6 mm is left solid. It is symmetric left to right.
  - **Depth:** the hollow's front face is flat at Y −32.8, so the face wall behind the flat black face is 3.2 mm thick (7.2 mm behind the letters). The hollow is open towards the back (Y 0).
- **Seam face and ledges:** the whole seam face is flat at Y 0. Where the front half is proud (its hump, the upper right end, the lower left end, and where only the back half dips), that part of the seam face is an exposed ledge with the 0.6 mm edge break on its outer edge.
- **Seam line:** where the two halves are flush (on the common shape), the outer edge of the rim has a small 0.6 mm 45 degree bevel. With the matching one on the back half it makes a neat V line.
- **Lip:** a strip 1.4 mm thick along the common shape, 1.8 to 3.2 mm in from its edge, continues past the seam by 4.0 mm, to Y +4.0, all the way round, so it slides into the back half. Its inner face is flush with the hollow.
  - **Why the lip is on the front half:** the halves are otherwise twins, so the joint works either way. Keeping it on the front half keeps the joint exactly as on the test piece (`CouponFront` has the lip), keeps the pry notch beside the lip as designed, and leaves the back half 4 mm lower on the plate.
- **Click bumps (4):** small ridges on the outer face of the lip. Each is 8 mm long along the wall, with its ends tapered over 1 mm.
  - **Profile across the ridge:** it stands 0.4 mm out from the lip. It is 1.4 mm wide at the base (Y +1.8 to +3.2) and has a flat top 0.6 mm wide (Y +2.2 to +2.8), with 45 degree sides.
  - **Placement:** two on the bottom wall, at about X −50 and X +50. One on each end wall of the common shape, where it runs straight up at about X ±98, centred at about Z 30. The common top has no straight stretch long enough for them away from corners and the slot.
  - **Spacing:** each bump is at least 10 mm from a corner, the slot and the pry notch.
- **Coin slot, front half:** a notch in the top wall from Y −2.5 to the seam at Y 0, through the full wall into the hollow.
  - **Place:** on the seam, centred at X 0, in the flat top over Б that both halves share (roughly X −17 to +17 at Z 84.96).
  - **Length rule:** the slot is the length of that shared flat top minus 2 mm, rounded down to a whole millimetre, at most 38 mm. Expected: **32 mm** (X −16 to +16). If it comes out below 30 mm, stop and report. Each end is rounded (radius 2.5 mm), so the two halves together make a slot with round ends.
  - **Why the end margin is only about 1 mm, not the earlier 4 mm:** beyond each end of the slot, one half's top rises into its hump (more material, not less), and the other half's top only starts its 8 mm rounded corner, dropping less than 0.3 mm in the first 2 mm.
  - **What passes:** a 26 mm coin clears the slot by about 3 mm at each end. Banknotes must be folded lengthwise to a strip no wider than about 28 mm; a note folded only in half does not fit.
  - **Lip:** over the slot's length plus 2 mm at each end (X −18 to +18), the lip is left out.
- **Pry notch:** a notch for a coin, in the bottom wall at the seam, centred at X 0, on the common part of the bottom. It is 14 mm long (along X), 2.4 mm wide (Y −2.4 to 0) and 1.5 mm deep (Z 0 to 1.5). It is cut in the front half only. A coin pushed in bears against the back half's rim, and twisting it pries the halves apart.

### 3.3 `BankBack` (back half, with the back name)

- **Outside:** exactly the outside of `BankFront` from its letter faces to the seam, without its lip and pry notch, turned half a turn (180 degrees) round the vertical line X 0, Y 0. So:
  - the letter faces are at Y +40, and read «АЙБАТ» left to right when you look at the back, with the first А on the viewer's left (+X);
  - the letter walls run straight forward 4.0 mm to the flat black face at Y +36, with the colour-boundary edge at Y +36.4;
  - the flat black face and its 0.6 mm edge break;
  - the back half's own outline (the front outline mirrored about X = 0) runs forward to the seam at Y 0. It has its hump at X +17.43 to +56.42, its leaning А end at the right (+X) and its Т step-in at the left (−X). It is solid in its proud regions and ends in flat ledges at Y 0.
- **Inside:** the same hollow as the front half (the common shape shrunk by 3.2 mm), with its back face flat at Y +32.8 (a 3.2 mm face wall). It is open towards the front.
- **Rebate for the lip:** the step in the wall that takes the front half's lip. Over the first 4.3 mm behind the seam (Y 0 to +4.3), only the outer 1.6 mm of the common-shape wall stays, as a thin skirt. Any proud solid of the back half outside the common shape stays as it is. The inner part is removed, which leaves a 0.2 mm gap round the lip's outer face. The lip stops 0.3 mm short of the floor of the step (Y +4.0 against +4.3), so the halves meet on the outer seam and not on the lip's end.
- **Click grooves (4):** in the skirt's inner face, opposite the 4 ridges. Each is 10 mm long and 0.3 mm deep.
  - **Profile:** it is 1.6 mm wide at the face (Y +1.7 to +3.3) and has a flat floor 1.0 mm wide (Y +2.0 to +3.0), with 45 degree sides.
  - **How it clicks:** when the halves close, each ridge has to push the skirt 0.2 mm outwards and then drops into its groove.
- **Coin slot, back half:** the matching notch, from the seam at Y 0 to Y +2.5. It has the same length, ends and place as in the front half. Over the slot's length plus 2 mm at each end, the rebate is filled solid (full wall thickness), because the lip is missing there.
- **Seam line and ledges:** the same 0.6 mm 45 degree bevel on the outer edge of the skirt's end where the halves are flush, and the same edge break on the outer edge of its own ledges.
- **No pry notch** on the back half.

### 3.4 Joint test piece (`CouponFront`, `CouponBack_015`, `CouponBack_020`, `CouponBack_025`)

The test piece is a small box with exactly the same joint as the money box, printed first to find the gap that clicks well. The joint did not change in these revisions, so the test piece is unchanged.

- **Outline:** a rectangle 60 x 30 mm with corners rounded to 6 mm.
- **Walls:** 3.2 mm. Floors: 2.4 mm.
- **`CouponFront`**
  - Floor on the plate, wall up to 10.0 mm, and then the lip, 1.4 mm thick and 4.0 mm tall, to 14.0 mm.
  - One click ridge centred on each long side.
  - A pry notch (14 x 2.4 x 1.5 mm) centred on one short side.
  - The 0.6 mm seam bevel.
- **`CouponBack_015`, `_020`, `_025`**
  - Floor on the plate, full wall up to 6.0 mm, then the skirt and step up to 10.3 mm.
  - One groove on each long side.
  - The 0.6 mm seam bevel.
- **The three backs differ only in the gap** between the lip and the skirt: 0.15, 0.20 and 0.25 mm. Their skirts are therefore 1.65, 1.60 and 1.55 mm thick. The grooves are 0.3 mm deep in each.
- **Labels:** each back carries its number, "15", "20" or "25", as raised digits on the inside of its floor. The digits are 8 mm tall, 0.6 mm high, with strokes at least 1.2 mm wide.
- **How it is used:** press `CouponFront` into each back in turn.
  - **The right one** closes by hand with a clear click, shows no gap at the seam, does not wobble, and opens with a coin in the pry notch.
  - **Applying the result:** the money box gets that gap. Its skirt is made thinner or thicker by the same difference before export.
  - **If two feel equally good**, take the tighter one. The long walls of the money box bend more easily than the walls of the small test piece.

## 6. Topology (how the surface is built)

- **Type:** hard-surface solids made for printing. Each object is one closed solid: watertight, no holes in the surface, no loose or doubled faces, no parts passing through themselves, normals pointing outwards.
- **Real size:** everything is built at the sizes above, with the scale applied (reset to 1).
- **No smoothing step** (no subdivision). Curved letter outlines are made of enough short straight pieces that no facet shows in the renders: no more than 0.05 mm off the true curve.
- **Size of the mesh:** about 50,000–200,000 triangles per half is fine.
- **Thinnest parts:**
  - nothing thinner than 1.2 mm anywhere. The thinnest parts are the 1.3 mm wall behind each click groove and the 1.4 mm lip;
  - the click ridge (0.4 mm high) and the groove (0.3 mm deep) are shapes on a wall, not free-standing details.
- **Colour boundary:** on both halves, the edge 3.6 mm behind the letter faces is a real edge round every letter wall and hole wall, so the faces in front of it can carry the green.
- No animation, no rig.

## 10. Print

- **What it is for, and the load:**
  - A money box on a desk or shelf. It stands on its bottom face and holds coins and folded notes.
  - Full, it may hold about 2–3 kg of coins (estimate). The weight rests on the bottom walls of both halves.
  - The lip inside the step stops the halves from sliding against each other up and down or sideways. The 4 clicks only hold them together.
  - It is a box for keeping money, not a lock: a child can open it with a coin.
  - Pick it up full by holding both halves, not by one half alone.
- **Printed size:**
  - assembled: 220 x 80 x 100 mm (W x D x H);
  - front half on the plate: 220 x 100 mm, 44 mm tall (40 mm plus the 4 mm lip);
  - back half on the plate: 220 x 100 mm, 40 mm tall;
  - each fits the 256 x 256 x 256 mm volume with about 18 mm to spare along the long side.
- **Plates, in order:**
  1. **Test piece** (`CouponFront`, `CouponBack_015`, `_020`, `_025`).
     - Grey, slot A2, one colour, no supports.
     - Each part lies floor-down.
     - Small and quick, about 15 g (estimate).
     - Its result sets the gap before the box is exported (3.4).
  2. **Front half** (`BankFront_Print`), letter faces down, with supports and one colour change.
  3. **Back half** (`BankBack_Print`), exactly the same way.
- **For both halves:**
  - **Bed face:** only the green letter faces and the breve touch the plate (Y −40 for the front half, Y +40 for the back half). They are large and flat and get the textured-plate finish.
  - **The colour change rule:** green (A1) from the plate; the slicer changes to black (A4) **two layers below the flat black face**, that is at the relief height minus two layer heights. With 0.2 mm layers and the 4.0 mm relief this is **3.6 mm**: green for layers from 0 to 3.6 mm, black from the layer that starts at 3.6 mm to the top. The model's colour-boundary edge sits at the same 3.6 mm. If a different layer height is chosen, the change and the boundary edge move to 4.0 mm minus two of those layers, and the modeler moves the edge to match.
  - **Why two layers early (the user's reason):** the last support layers (their top interface) and the last layers of the letter walls are then already black, so the black face is not smeared with green from the supports. The 0.4 mm black at the base of each letter is not noticeable from the front.
  - **Layer height** must make both 3.6 mm and 4.0 mm layer boundaries, for example 0.2 mm layers with a 0.2 mm first layer.
  - **Supports are required** under the whole flat black face of each half's own outline, which lies 4.0 mm above the plate. That includes:
    - the border round the letters and the 3 mm gaps between them;
    - the insides of А and Б;
    - the gap between the breve and Й;
    - the face of the hump and of the other proud regions.

    All supports stand on the plate; nothing inside the box needs support. The user's point, recorded: the supports are printed during the green layers, so they come out green (with black top layers) and cost no extra filament change or purge.
  - **For the print engineer to decide:**
    - the support type (normal or tree);
    - the interface layers and their pattern;
    - the top Z gap between the support and the black face, which decides how cleanly the face releases and how rough it is;
    - whether the narrow 3 mm gaps and the small closed letter holes can be bridged instead of supported.

    Pulling supports out of the closed holes of А and Б is the awkward part of the clean-up; a support pattern that comes out in one piece helps.
  - **Seam ledges print without supports.** Each half lies with its seam face on top, so every ledge is part of the last, upward-facing layers of that half. In the assembled box the ledges are visible flat steps, and they come out smooth.
  - **Proud regions** are solid in the model. The slicer fills them with its normal sparse infill.
  - If the slicer adds a purge tower for the colour change, the half lies so that the tower fits beside its 100 mm side.
  - Both halves change colour at the same height, so they could share one plate (about 220 x 210 mm together). Version 1 plans one plate each: then a failed plate costs only one half, and the purge tower has room. The print engineer may suggest one shared plate to the user.
- **Surface finish, accepted by the user on 2026-11-13:** the flat black faces are printed over supports, so they do not get the plate texture and will show support marks. Only the letter faces get the plate texture. This was accepted in exchange for straight letters.
- **Filament estimate** (rough; the print engineer gives the real figures). All Bambu PLA Lite, loaded as A1 green, A2 grey, A3 white, A4 black:
  - the solid volume is expected to rise from the 340 cm³ measured on the symmetric version to about 400–450 cm³. The hollow shrinks to the common shape, and the proud regions are solid. The outer surfaces shrink a little because there is no plateau. The printed weight rises less than the volume, because the slicer fills solid regions sparsely;
  - black (A4): about 280–360 g for both halves, including the black top layers of the supports;
  - green (A1): about 45–75 g for both halves: about 30–40 g in the letters and about 15–35 g in the supports, which are thrown away. The black faces are smaller than on the symmetric version, so there are fewer supports;
  - grey (A2): about 15 g for the test piece;
  - white (A3): not used;
  - plus the waste of the two colour changes (purge), a few grams to about 15 g each (estimate).
- **Overhangs:** in print orientation, the only surfaces facing the plate are the letter faces (on the plate) and the flat black face with its edge break (on the supports). Everything else is upright, faces up, or leans no more than 45 degrees:
  - the outline sides and the proud regions are upright walls;
  - the ledges face up;
  - the click ridges and the sides of the grooves are at 45 degrees;
  - the slot and the pry notch are open at the top of the rim;
  - there is no ceiling inside either half.
- **Walls:** outer walls of the common shape 3.2 mm, face wall 3.2 mm behind each flat black face, lip 1.4 mm, skirt 1.6 mm, at least 1.3 mm behind a groove; proud regions solid.
- **Fits and gaps, starting values:**

  | Fit | Starting value | Tuned by |
  |---|---|---|
  | Lip to skirt, sideways | 0.20 mm | test piece: 0.15 / 0.20 / 0.25 |
  | Lip end to the floor of the step | 0.30 mm | |

  | Slot | 5.0 mm wide, 32 mm long, for coins up to 26 mm across and 2.5 mm thick, and notes folded to 28 mm | |
  | Pry notch | 2.4 mm wide, for a coin edge | |

- **May not be changed without the user:**
  - the font, the name, the overall size, the name on both sides, the outline following each side's letters;
  - the straight letter walls, the 4.0 mm relief, and the colour change two layers below the black face;
  - the split at Y 0, the slot size and position, the colours.
  - Turning either half to another face defeats the design. Report instead.
- **For the print engineer:**
  - The click joint needs solid walls near the rim. Four or more wall loops make the 3.2 mm walls nearly solid.
  - The letter faces of each half must touch the plate fully: flat, not tilted.
  - Whether `slice_model` can insert the filament change at 3.6 mm and set up the supports is not known. Either may have to be set in the Bambu Studio window, for two plates. This is a question for the orchestrator and the user, not a reason to change the model.

## 13. What a later version could add

- **A longer slot (option, needs the user's choice):** turn the slot to run across the top, front to back, instead of along the seam. It would be 5 mm wide (in X) and up to 38 mm long (in Y), centred at X 0 over Б, where both halves share a flat top 72 mm deep.
  - Each half gets half of it as a notch open at the seam, so it prints without bridging.
  - The lip is interrupted for only about 9 mm.
  - It passes a 26 mm coin and notes folded only in half (up to about 36 mm wide).
  - It looks less like the references, which have the slot along the top.
- **Rejected alternative, kept for the record:** one shared outline, symmetric left to right, with a flat plateau over the middle three letters. It had no steps at the seam and allowed a 38 mm slot, but the outline no longer followed the letters. The user chose the per-side outline from its renders on 2026-11-13.
- **A textured black face as well:**
  - letters as separate inserts: the black body prints with its flat face down on the plate, with pockets for the letters, and the green letters print separately face down and are pressed or glued in. Both colours get the plate texture and no supports are needed;
  - a flush two-colour face: green letters inlaid level with a flat black face, printed face down with the AMS swapping colours inside each layer;
  - the support-free 45 degree bevel of the first draft, if the support marks bother the user.
- Both halves on one plate, if the purge tower fits, to save one colour change.
- A thin white outline round the letters, or the black face in a third colour.
- An engraved line on the bottom: a date, "from ...", or a small picture.
- A one-way flap under the slot so coins do not shake out, or a round plug in the bottom instead of the split.
- Small rubber feet or a printed non-slip pad.
- Magnets in the rim instead of the click bumps, if the clicks wear out.
- A rendered plate texture on the letter faces, so the renders show the real finish.
- Another font, if Impact turns out too heavy in the renders.
